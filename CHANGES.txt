LANTERNLIGHT - CHANGES FROM THE DESIGN DOCUMENT
================================================

The core of the one-pager made it in as designed: a single-screen lighthouse,
a mouse-aimed beam that burns shadows, oil that drains while the beam is on,
oil barrels hauled in by shining on them, three flames of health, Crawlers,
Skitters and Brutes, one Flare per night, five nights, and the
title -> night -> dawn -> victory / game over -> retry night flow.

These are the things that changed while building and playtesting it.


GAMEPLAY

1. Oil refills slowly while the beam is off.
   In the design, oil only came from barrels. In playtesting, running dry
   was a death spiral: no light meant no way to reach barrels, so the night
   was lost. Now the tank trickles back while the beam rests, which turns oil
   into a rhythm of short bursts and rests instead of one big reserve.

2. New enemy: the Wisp (nights 4-5).
   A flying shadow that ignores the rocks and weaves toward the lamp itself.
   It forces the player to aim up into the sky instead of only along the ground.

3. Dawn blessings (upgrades between nights).
   This was listed as a stretch goal. After each dawn the player picks one of
   two random blessings: Wide Beam, Deep Tank, Hotter Flame, Spare Wick
   (+1 max flame), Second Flare, or Steady Wick (beam uses less oil).

4. Flames carry over between nights, and dawn relights one.
   The design didn't say what happens to flames between nights. Carrying them
   over (with a one-flame heal at sunrise) makes every lost flame matter more.

5. Brutes put out two flames instead of one.
   Without this they were easy to ignore. Shadows also slow down while lit,
   Brutes less than others.

6. Surges.
   On later nights a warning appears and packs of shadows attack both sides at
   once, which is when the Flare becomes important.

7. Night lengths vary from 65 s to 95 s (the design said about 90 s each).
   Shorter early nights get the player into the action faster.

8. Score, burn streaks and a best score.
   Not in the design. Killing shadows in quick succession builds a streak
   multiplier (up to x3). Barrels and surviving dawn give bonus points.
   The best score is saved between sessions.

9. Retrying a night restores the exact state from the start of that night
   (score, flames and blessings), so retries are fair.


SOUND

10. No looping background music.
    The design listed a title theme, a tense night loop and a dawn sting.
    Following the assignment's rule about music, the loops were replaced by
    music that comes from the gameplay:
      - each kill in a burn streak plucks the next note up a pentatonic scale,
      - a bell melody climbs one note every 10% of the night toward dawn,
      - a low drone fades in as shadows get close to the lighthouse,
      - a heartbeat plays when only one flame is left,
      - a lighthouse bell tolls at the start of each night.
    The dawn sting stayed (now a warm chord as the sun rises).

11. Extra creature sounds: each enemy type announces itself (Skitter
    chittering, Brute roar, Wisp whisper), and spawn splashes are panned
    left or right so you can hear which side shadows are coming from.

12. All sounds were synthesized with my own script (Tools/generate_audio.py)
    instead of jsfxr plus free libraries.


ART

13. All art is original pixel art generated by a script
    (Tools/generate_art.py) instead of downloaded assets: sky with a dawn
    version, twinkling stars, drifting clouds, animated sea, rocky island,
    lighthouse with a glowing lantern and halo, the beam, four animated
    shadow creatures with a burn-glow layer, barrels, particles and HUD icons.

14. A custom 5x7 pixel font is used for all text so the UI matches the art.

15. The sky gradually warms toward sunrise in the last third of each night.


UI AND FLOW

16. Title screen with how-to-play, and the beam sweeps the sky on its own
    there like a real lighthouse.

17. Night cards ("NIGHT 3") with a one-line hint about what is new that night.

18. Pause menu (Esc) with quit to title.

19. First-night tutorial popup on the first oil barrel, and a "SPACE - FLARE!"
    reminder the first time the player gets swarmed each night.


TECHNICAL

20. The whole game is built from code when Play is pressed (Bootstrap.cs),
    so there are no scene objects or prefabs to wire up by hand.

21. Beam hit detection works as planned in the design doc: an angle check
    between the beam direction and the target, plus a range check.

22. Balancing was done with a headless playtest bot (Tools/Playtest) that
    plays the real game scripts at several skill levels and reports flames lost,
    oil usage and game overs per night.
